## Blackbeard's Log — Complete

---

### Entry One
*Thaumont 3rd, 831 AC*

*Two weeks in this accursed hole and I begin to understand why the compass brought us here. The instrument is not broken as Mares first suggested — I had him flogged for the suggestion and I stand by it. It responds. Not to north, not to any fixed point I can determine by conventional means, but to something older. I have been watching it in relation to the sky chart on the cabin wall — the one I took from the Milenian scholar off the coast of Thyatis, the one I kept because something about it nagged at me.*

*The needle moves in relation to the constellations. I am certain of it now. When I orient the chart to the chamber ceiling — and the ceiling here rewards careful study, more than I first gave it credit for — the compass responds. It is trying to tell me something.*

*The dragon has not approached the ship again since Mares. Perhaps the smell of blood satisfies it for now. I do not intend to test this theory.*

---

### Entry Two
*Thaumont 7th, 831 AC*

*I have been studying the rim inscription for three days. My Milenian is passable but this is old — older than anything I have encountered. I will record what I have.*

*KYROS AETHON MARIS ETERNUM — this defeats me partially. Kyros I think means breath or spirit. Aethon I cannot place. Maris is sea. Eternum is plain enough. Something like "by the eternal breath of the sea" perhaps.*

*PER SIGNUM HORAE ET GRADUUM — this is clear. "By the sign of the hour and the degrees." The degrees must be spoken aloud here — the correct heading, calculated as I have described above. In my case: Nonaginta. Ninety.*

*OSTIUM APERIAT — "let the door open." Plain enough.*

*NAVIS TRANSEAT — "let the ship pass." Plainer still.*

*IN LUCEM SEMPER — "into the light, always."*

*I believe the full incantation must be spoken aloud by whoever holds the compass, at the correct heading, above the heat. The degrees spoken in the old tongue — I have written the Milenian numerals on the chart for reference. Nonaginta for ninety. The speaker must supply their own number.*

*The word I cannot translate is Aethon. It may not matter. It may matter enormously. I have run out of time to determine which.*

---

### Entry Three
*Thaumont 8th, 831 AC*

*A critical note on the setting of the compass.*

*I made this error once and will not make it again. The compass rose on the Milenian chart is old and finely graduated — one degree from another is a hair's breadth on that paper. I set the instrument by eye on my first attempt and felt nothing. No vibration. No response. An error of perhaps three or four degrees was enough to render the whole calculation dead.*

*The sextant corrects this. Place the chart flat. Set the sextant's arc to your calculated heading — Nonaginta in my case — and use it to transfer that angle precisely onto the compass setting. The instrument was built for exactly this kind of angular measurement. Do not trust your eye. Trust the sextant.*

*Precision is everything. We are not approximating a harbour mouth. We are opening a door between worlds.*

---

### Entry Four
*Thaumont 9th, 831 AC*

*I have it. Or the bones of it at least.*

*A navigator divides his world into three hundred and sixty degrees. The celestial scholars divide their sky into twelve equal houses. The arithmetic is not complicated — a child could do it, which is perhaps why it took me six days.*

*Twelve houses. Three hundred and sixty degrees. Each house therefore commands thirty degrees of the sky — no more, no less. Fixed. Immutable. The compass knows this. The compass has always known this.*

*The question is not what degree corresponds to what house. That is simple enough to calculate — I have done so and annotated the Milenian chart accordingly, the workings are in the margin. The question is which house to begin from.*

*I have been a sailor long enough to know that a heading means nothing without a reference point. True east is always east. But which house sits at east?*

*The answer, I think, is not fixed. I think it moves. I think it is always the house of the current month — the house of right now, of this sky, of this season. The compass is a navigator's instrument. It speaks a navigator's language. It wants a heading. Give it the right one and I believe — I am nearly certain — it will open the door.*

*I need one more day to confirm it.*

---

### Entry Five
*Thaumont 11th, 831 AC*

*Thaumont. The third month. Thirty degrees to the house.*

*Three times thirty.*

*Ninety degrees. Due east. The oldest heading in the world — into the rising sun, into the new day, into the beginning of things. Of course. Of course it is.*

*I have set the compass. I can feel it working — a vibration in the instrument that was not there before, a hum that I feel in my back teeth rather than hear with my ears. The portal will open. I know it will open. I know exactly where it will deliver us — the chart shows the corresponding point with precision and I have marked it in red.*

*But the beast is back.*

*It came through the fissure an hour ago and has not left. It circles the ship. It knows something has changed — perhaps it feels the compass as I do, perhaps it simply smells my certainty. I have ordered the men to silence. We wait.*

*If whoever reads this is not one of my crew — if you have found your way down here by your own means, by your own stubbornness, by whatever ill fortune or singular purpose drove you into this hole — then know this.*

*The compass will take you home. The mathematics are on the chart. Do not make my mistake. Do not wait for a better moment.*

*There is no better moment. There is only now, or never.*

*Set your heading. Open the door. Move.*

*— J.B.*
